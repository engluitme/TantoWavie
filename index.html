The Story

Born Shingirai Filbert Makaza in Chitungwiza, Zimbabwe, Tanto Wavie grew up in Zengeza with a pull toward performance long before the music took shape. He found his way into hip-hop, but rather than settle into an imported sound, he built something of his own: TrapSu, a genre that welds trap production to Sungura, the guitar-driven style that's been part of Zimbabwe's musical backbone for generations.

The breakthrough came with a tribute track to a Sungura legend that introduced a wider audience to what TrapSu could sound like. Since then, he's carried the movement across a run of albums and EPs, self-producing much of his catalogue and pushing the genre onto festival stages, including a headline slot at Shoko Festival. A 2018 Zimbabwe Hip Hop Awards newcomer nomination marked the genre's arrival — the releases since have kept building on it.
